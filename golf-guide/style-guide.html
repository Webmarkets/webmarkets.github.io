The Golf Guide style guide

Twenty years of bringing golfers together deserves a site that feels like a well-made golf magazine: warm paper, quiet type, and photography that makes you want to book the trip. This is the reference for every page we build.

01

A small palette that stays out of the way of the photography. Green leads, cream is the ground, and brass appears only in small doses: ranking numbers, underlines, and the occasional tag.

Typography

Buttons, links and tags

Buttons are rectangular with a whisper of rounding, in the sans family at normal size. No pills, no uppercase tracking, no arrows bolted onto every label. In-copy links carry a brass underline.

Tags label cards with their category or date. They sit below or beside content, never above a heading pretending to be an eyebrow.

04

Photography

Pictures are the point. Real courses, real members, real weather. Landscape crops at 3:2 for cards and features, 4:3 for supporting shots, and 21:9 for full-width banners. Every image on an editorial page gets a caption: it is the fastest way to look like a publication rather than a template.

Caption style: set in the small size, stone colour, above a hairline rule. Name the course, the hole if you know it, and the trip.

Photo overlay standard

For banners and feature cards with text over an image, use the pine scrim from the bottom only. Text stays cream, and the scrim never climbs past the midpoint of the picture.

06

Voice and usage

The site should read like it was written by the people who run the tours, because it is. These rules keep it that way.

Always

  • Sentence case headings, UK English spelling throughout
  • Name real places, real courses, real dates
  • Caption photographs like a magazine would
  • Short sentences, plain words, prices stated up front
  • One green button per section, doing one clear job

Never

  • Em dashes. Use a comma, a full stop, or a colon
  • Eyebrow or kicker labels above headings
  • Gradient text, glows, or glassy blur panels
  • Words like elevate, seamless, unlock, or empower
  • Block capitals with wide letter spacing
