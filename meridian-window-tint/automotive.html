Why Tint Your Vehicle

A Cooler Cabin

Film rejects solar heat before it builds up inside, so your A/C has less work to do after the car sits in a parking lot.

Less Glare

Easier on your eyes at sunrise, at sunset and under headlights. That makes for a more comfortable drive on I-84.

UV Protection for You

Window film blocks most of the UV that comes through side and rear glass. That’s skin protection on every commute.

Protects Your Interior

UV and heat are what fade seats and crack dashboards. Film slows that down and helps protect resale value.

Privacy and Security

Darker shades keep what’s in your back seat out of plain view. Film can also help hold broken glass together.

A Clean, Factory Look

Professional install, no bubbles, no peeling edges and no color shift. [CONFIRM: “won’t fade” applies to the films they carry]

What’s Legal in Idaho

We follow Idaho’s limits on every install. VLT is the percentage of light that gets through. Lower numbers mean darker tint.

WindowMinimum VLTMax reflectivity
WindshieldNon-reflective, above the AS-1 line only—
Front side windows35% (±3%)35% (±3%)
Rear side windows20% (±3%)35% (±3%)
Rear window35% (±3%)35% (±3%)

Medical exemption

With a physician’s written verification kept in the vehicle, Idaho allows 70% on the windshield and 20% on other windows.

Factory tint

Film over factory privacy glass adds up. We measure first and tell you what’s legal.

Out-of-state plates

Each state has its own rules. We’ll go over yours before we start.

Based on Idaho Code § 49-944. Laws can change. This is general information, not legal advice.

Auto Tint FAQs

How dark can I tint my windows in Idaho?

Front side windows and the rear window must let in at least 35% of light. Rear side windows can go to 20%. Windshield tint is only allowed above the AS-1 line.
