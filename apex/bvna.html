Vertebrogenic Pain

Pain from inside the bone needs a different fix.

Vertebrogenic pain is a distinct type of chronic low back pain caused by damage to the vertebral endplates — the interface between the disc and the vertebral body. Everyday wear-and-tear and disc degeneration stress these endplates, and the resulting inflammation shows up on MRI as "Modic changes." Those pain signals travel to the brain along one nerve inside the vertebra: the basivertebral nerve. BVNA disrupts that nerve at its source — you may have seen it called Intracept®, the original brand of this procedure; at Apex we perform the same proven approach.

  • Minimally invasive and outpatient — you go home the same day
  • Implant-free, no fusion, and no sutures or staples — future treatment options stay open
  • Performed under sedation with fluoroscopic (X-ray) guidance
  • Shown to improve function and relieve pain long-term, with many patients reporting durable relief
Is This You?

The tell-tale pattern.

  • Deep, aching pain in the middle of your low back
  • Pain that stays in your back — not down your leg
  • Prolonged sitting makes it worse
  • Physical activity aggravates it
  • Relief when lying down, standing, or walking
  • Bending forward or lifting sets it off
  • Standing up from a chair triggers it
  • Your MRI mentions "Modic changes"

If months — or years — of physical therapy, chiropractic, medications, injections, or other procedures haven't fixed it, you may be a candidate for BVNA. Bring your MRI to your consultation and we'll check for Modic changes together.

What to Expect

Three simple steps.

01

Diagnosis

We review your symptoms and treatment history, then use MRI to confirm vertebrogenic pain — looking for the endplate inflammation that appears as Modic changes.

02

The Procedure

Under sedation or general anesthesia, a needle is guided by X-ray through a tiny incision into the vertebral body. A specialized probe is then heated to disrupt the pain signals from the basivertebral nerve. It can be repeated at other affected levels, and no sutures or staples are required.

03

Recovery & Follow-Up

It's a same-day, outpatient procedure — and because it's implant-free, your future treatment options stay open. We follow up to track your relief and function.

The goal is reduced pain and improved function, and many patients report durable, long-term relief. As with any procedure, there are risks — overall, a very low rate of adverse events has been reported, and we'll walk through all of them with you at your consultation.
